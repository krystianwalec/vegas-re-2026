Key decision

WAPA grid tie vs. off-grid + generator

Any architecture is acceptable. Pick the path that stays seamless for guests and has the lowest total cost, including rental impact, over 5, 7, and 10 years.

A local St. Thomas architect advised against Tesla Powerwalls because another system integrates better with generators. The current lean is to connect to the WAPA grid and skip generators — partly because a new service run is already ~150 ft from the road — but that is a trade to test on the floor, not a settled choice.

1. Any solution is fine

Grid + battery, off-grid + auto-start generator, or hybrid (grid + battery + generator). Leave Tuesday with one shortlist, not a locked brand.

2. Seamless for guests

No overnight outages and no house rules like “set the AC to 78 when you leave.” If a generator is in the design, it has to auto-start and carry the full load, including AC.

3. Lowest 5 / 7 / 10-year cost

Compare total cost of ownership, including equipment, shipping, WAPA or generator operating costs, battery replacement, and lost rental revenue from outages or guest restrictions.

Reference house: On The Rocks, St. John

Andy is staying at On The Rocks (408 Chocolate Hole) — a new 5-bedroom finished in 2025, about 2,800 sq ft living / 5,000 sq ft built, designed for 165 mph winds. The listing says solar plus Powerwall backup. From the architect’s roof plan and the 3D drawing on the house site, the array is about 18–19 panels, roughly 7–8 kW (panel wattage is a guess). The owner says the house is off-grid, with a backup generator, and asks guests to turn all 6 AC units to 78 when leaving each day so enough energy is stored for overnight cooling. If power does go out, “it usually comes back on around nine or 10am” as the panels recharge. A working automatic generator that can run the AC should not let the house go dark — so the generator may not auto-start, may be too small, or may not be wired to the whole house. The 2025 drawings also show a WAPA meter and transformer; Andy is checking whether a utility feed was ever connected. Takeaway for our ~4,000 sq ft rentals: a small array plus an unreliable generator produces guest rules and possible lights-out nights. We need a larger array and/or more efficient cooling, and we should not count on a generator unless it auto-starts and carries the full load.

Tesla’s current generator support (verified Nov 11, 2026): Official Tesla docs still say Powerwall 3 systems must have a grid connection — fully off-grid is not supported. The compatibility table lists automatic generator control: No and charging from an upstream generator: No. A downstream generator can be added in a cascading ATS setup (application note Rev 4.0, Jan 15, 2025, updated for Powerwall 3 and Gateway 3): Tesla does not start or stop the generator; the generator powers loads after Powerwall is depleted or overloaded, and it does not recharge the batteries. After the ATS takes over, the Powerwall Enable switch must be toggled before Powerwall can resume and charge from solar. No 2025–2026 Tesla announcement of native auto-start or generator charging turned up. The Caribbean warranty (effective May 6, 2026) explicitly covers the U.S. Virgin Islands for Powerwall 3 and Gateway 3 (10 years; internet needed for the full term). So the architect’s concern still holds if a generator is part of the design. If we stay grid-tied with no generator, Tesla remains in the running.
Current lean

Grid + battery backup

  • Pros: No generator fuel, noise, maintenance, or auto-start failure. WAPA covers multi-day shortfalls and poor solar days. Tesla is a supported path. Simpler for absentee owners if batteries and monitoring are solid. Guest experience can be seamless if batteries cover typical outages.
  • Cons: Pay for the ~150 ft service run (trenching, conduit, possibly a transformer) plus interconnection and ongoing WAPA bills, including the monthly customer charge. Under Net Energy Billing, exports are credited at only 75% of LEAC — much less than retail — so batteries matter for self-consumption. WAPA has been unreliable; batteries must be sized for outages, not just daily cycling. A long or post-hurricane outage can still exceed battery capacity with no generator fallback. Interconnection time is not guaranteed.

Off-grid + auto-start generator

  • Pros: Avoids the 150 ft WAPA extension, interconnection process, and ongoing utility bills. Full independence. Victron, Sol-Ark, SMA Sunny Island, Enphase, FranklinWH, and Generac document native auto-start and can charge batteries from the generator.
  • Cons: Tesla Powerwall 3 is not supported off-grid. The generator must auto-start and carry the whole house, AC included, or guests get On The Rocks-style outages. Fuel, maintenance, noise, and emissions on a rental. Solar and storage must be oversized to hit 80–90% without a grid to lean on. A failed generator or empty tank is a hard outage. TCO has to include fuel, service, and any rental loss.

Hybrid: grid + battery + generator

  • Pros: Best resilience — grid for ordinary shortfalls, batteries for typical outages, auto-start generator for extended or hurricane events. Native generator integration from Victron, Sol-Ark, Enphase, FranklinWH, Generac, and SMA Sunny Island. The strongest fit for a seamless guest stay.
  • Cons: Highest equipment and complexity cost (pay for the 150 ft feed and a generator). Tesla can only add a downstream ATS generator that does not charge the batteries and is not Tesla-controlled. More maintenance. This path wins on TCO only if it prevents enough rental losses or if WAPA is worse than expected.

Local installer (ProSolar already does Tesla and Enphase on STT) handles shipping, DPNR permitting, WAPA interconnection, and service.
